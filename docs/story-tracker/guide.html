How to build a story

In shortPick the story in the tracker, read its brief, plan briefly with Claude against this page, then build on a branch from dev. A processor turns DBIE files into a frozen data.gen.ts; the page reads only that. The tracker updates itself when the branch opens a pull request (WIP) and when it merges (On dev).

  1. Start from the brief. Open the story's row below: the headline figures, chapters, visual, interactions, data sources and fact-check traps are there, with overlaps and links to other stories. Ask Claude: "Read docs/story-tracker/stories.json entry Sn and docs/story-tracker/guide.html, then plan the build." Planning should take minutes, not days.
  2. Read what it absorbs. If the entry lists absorbs (e.g. S18 absorbs S12), read those entries' briefs too: they are now chapters of this story, and their research, debates and fact-check traps apply to those chapters. mayAbsorb marks a merge still to be agreed with the other story's owner. Read the overlaps and links lines and, for any live story they name, its row in tracker.json, so the new story links to it rather than repeating it.
  3. Branch from dev. git switch -c story-<slug> origin/dev. The slug is the story's address: /stories/<slug>, lower case, words joined by hyphens, read as a title (e.g. money-in-money-out).
  4. Restore the data. pnpm data:fetch brings back the latest scrape (data/sdmx, data/reports); pnpm data:pull the site's release JSON. Never commit scraped files.
  5. Write the processor. data/processors/story-<slug>.mjs, modelled on story-money-in-money-out.mjs: a header comment listing every DBIE series code, file and unit used, any non-DBIE source (kept in data/sources/*.json with its citation) and every check; it reads the scraped files with readSdmxCsv from scripts/db/lib.mjs, writes the oracle data/processors/out/story-<slug>.json, the page's src/app/stories/<slug>/data.gen.ts and downloads under public/stories/<slug>/, and fails without writing if a check does not hold. Add it to the list in data/processors/build.mjs.
  6. Comment the data. In data.gen.ts, the processor writes one comment line above each export saying what it is, its unit and period (e.g. "Private transfers received, US$ billion, by financial year."). The tracker reads these lines to list what the story shows.
  7. Build the page. src/app/stories/<slug>/page.tsx with metadata.title "<Title> | Stories — Database on Indian Economy" and a one-sentence description with the headline figures; page.client.tsx and components beside it; styles in <slug>.css. Every number on the page comes from data.gen.ts; no figure is typed into copy by hand. Reuse the shared pieces (section rail, timeline scrubber, chart kits) from the live stories.
  8. List it. Add the story to src/app/stories/(dashboard)/sections.ts under its heading (the Stories page and the crumbs read it). A renamed story keeps a permanent redirect in next.config.js; the tracker follows redirects to keep its history.
  9. Preview and check. Run the site locally and check desktop and phone widths, dark mode and every number against the brief and the fact-check gates below. Show it to the story's reviewer before opening the pull request.
  10. Pull request to dev. Sentence-case title, no AI attribution. Opening it marks the story WIP here; merging marks it On dev, with you as the author of the update; reaching main marks it Live.

R

House rules for every story

In shortRBIH is a public institution and people will quote these pages. Every story reads as a sober, consulting-grade note: action-title headers with exact figures, one line of text, sources and method at the foot, no commentary and no causal claims.

  • Headers are action titles

    Each header states the whole message with an exact, data-backed figure ("2.2x", not a rounded "2.5x"), and each leads into the next. Titles short: one figure, one message, about 70 characters at most.

  • No eyebrows or kickers

    No small uppercase labels above headers. Section structure goes in a marker such as the section rail of "The phone pays, but the cash stays".

  • Little body text

    One line under each header plus a source line. No metaphors or loose wording ("tide", "the rain began", "race"), no leading statements.

  • Vary the layout

    Words left, right and on top across sections; never the same split throughout.

  • Sources footer

    In the format of "What ₹1 lakh could buy": SOURCES and DOWNLOADS (CSV files) in one column, METHOD in the other.

  • Words to avoid

    Never "dearer" (say "more expensive"); no weakness framing of the rupee ("lost half its value", "the fall everyone sees"); no loaded words (crisis, boom, distress, bubble, bad loans, clean-up); name every quantity precisely ("dollars that were worth ₹100 in April 2004").

  • RBI products and operations

    Word with extreme care. Quote the RBI for any cause; use official counts for headline multiples; never imply causation or intent. Stories that describe RBI's own operations get a courtesy review.

  • Groups, not names

    Banks, companies and states are shown as groups or as maps of facts; no individual bank or company singled out, no ranking words.
